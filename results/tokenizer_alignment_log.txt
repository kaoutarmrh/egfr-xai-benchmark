run_17_tokenizer_alignment.py has since been extended to record, per molecule,
the MINIMUM and MAXIMUM number of tokens covering an atom and the MAXIMUM
number of atoms spanned by any mapped token, and to print an explicit verdict
on whether the mapping is strictly one-to-one. This log and the accompanying
tokenizer_alignment.csv come from the earlier version and do not contain those
columns. Re-running the script regenerates both with them.

What the published files do establish, without a re-run:

  every row has atoms_without_token = 0   -> each atom has at least one token
  every row has mean_tokens_per_atom = 1.000
  the largest molecule has 103 heavy atoms

One extra token anywhere in a molecule would raise that molecule's mean to at
least 1 + 1/103 = 1.010, which cannot round to 1.000. At least one token per
atom, and an average of exactly one, therefore leaves EXACTLY ONE token per
atom in every molecule. That is the claim made in Section 2.4 of the paper, and
it is checkable from tokenizer_alignment.csv alone.

What they do NOT establish is the converse: whether any single token spans more
than one atom. The paper makes no claim about it. The new MAX columns settle it
directly.
